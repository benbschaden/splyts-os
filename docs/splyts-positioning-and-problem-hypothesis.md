# Splyts Positioning and Problem Hypothesis

## Executive Summary

We ran the first round of beta interviews across seven athletes — 4 active beta users, one who didn't activate (also friend connection), and two reference conversations for coach and Garmin-user perspectives. Going in, our null hypothesis claimed HYROX athletes on Apple Watch need split tracking, capacity understanding, and AI coaching to replace a $200/month human coach at 10x value.

**Most of that hypothesis is now falsified.** Split tracking and capacity understanding didn't come up as pain points. The "replace your coach" framing attracts the wrong customers — our strongest adopter doesn't think about Splyts that way. Only one user out of seven validated the full adaptive-coaching thesis — but she validated it completely.

**Our strongest adopter is Krysty** — a 40-year-old self-coached pharmacist in LA, training 6x/week for her first solo HYROX, using Splyts 2x daily. She describes the product unprompted in the exact language of its thesis: *"integrates sleep and HRV into the plan so it adapts as you go along."* She's not a casual beginner. She's a committed athlete new to HYROX — which, it turns out, is the real target.

**What this round taught us:**

- Users with coaches or class-based programming don't activate (Andre, Austin, Anthony, Martin)
- The intelligence layer is real, but it has to be experienced, not pitched
- Trust in AI coaching is uneven — we need to position around it carefully
- "HYROX mode on Apple Watch" and "training intelligence" were both wrong framings

**Our new positioning:** *Splyts is the training system for athletes who run and lift. It reads your body — your sleep, your recovery, your training — and builds a plan that adapts to you every day.*

**Our new null hypothesis** commits us to nine specific, falsifiable claims across acquisition, activation, and monetization — each grounded in what survived this round. The next experiment: targeted acquisition for more Krysty-types (committed, self-coached, Apple Watch, HYROX race booked), with clear falsification triggers for whether she's a segment or an outlier.

# The Five Assumptions We Set Out to Break

Going into this round, our null hypothesis was: *HYROX athletes training for races, with Apple Watch Series 4+, lack a way to track splits, understand capacity, or get adaptive training without a human coach. They cobble together generic fitness apps, spreadsheets, and community advice. Splyts' AI coach replaces the $200/month human coach at $25–30/month — 10x value. Real-time split tracking and AI pacers are the wedge.*

Below is each assumption stress-tested against the seven interviews.

## What Survived Falsification

After running every assumption against the data, here's what's left standing:

1. **HYROX-training athletes on Apple Watch are a reachable audience** — confirmed, but not meaningful on its own
2. **A specific athlete profile values adaptive training without a coach** — Krysty, and it's narrow
3. **The intelligence layer of the product is real but must be experienced, not pitched**
4. **Committed athletes new to HYROX are the right fit** — not casuals, not established competitors

---

## Assumption 1 — HYROX competitors training for races, with Apple Watch Series 4+

**Status: Not falsified, but weak signal.**

- Correct for all except one who did not adopt
- Weak as a claim, because this audience was pre-filtered by our original Reddit post and invite list. The sample tells us they exist and can be reached, but nothing more
- Essentially tautological: "People who signed up for our HYROX beta were HYROX athletes on Apple Watches"

---

## Assumption 2 — They can't track splits, understand capacity, or get adaptive training without a human coach

**Status: Two sub-claims, both largely falsified.**

### Part A — "Can't track splits, understand capacity"

**Falsified.** Did not come up as a problem for anyone.

### Part B — "Can't get adaptive training without a human coach"

**True for only 1 person — our best adopter.**

- **Krysty:** explicitly values adaptive training based on body state. Active user, 2x/day engagement
- **Austin:** said in theory he'd replace a $200 coach for $30, but never activated — weak signal
- **Andre:** has a coach, explicitly said he wouldn't trust AI without a human
- **Martin:** has gym classes + €40/month budget ceiling, doesn't need adaptive coaching
- **Anthony:** has his own gym classes, explicitly said he doesn't care about programming
- **Alex, Dave:** didn't engage with this feature at all

**Where this claim seems to hold:** someone who (a) doesn't have coach access, (b) doesn't have a class structure, (c) trusts technology to make training decisions, and (d) is data-literate enough to engage with adaptation signals. That's Krysty — and it's a narrow profile.

---

## Assumption 3 — Users see Splyts as a fitness app, not a training intelligence system

**Status: Partially supported, but the more interesting finding is nuanced.**

**What users actually call Splyts in their own words:**

- **Andre:** *"A very complete hybrid coaching app, flexible at the end of the day"*
- **Krysty:** *"Like Runna but more customizable, integrates sleep and HRV into the plan that adapts"*
- **Anthony:** *"A functional tool for hybrid-style athletes, more than Strava"*
- **Martin:** *"A hybrid workout app — best available for what it does"*
- **Alex:** *"A HYROX training and plan-building app"*
- **Dave:** *"An AI tool that schedules your training and monitors"*
- **Austin:** *"A Whoop mixed with everything else — one-stop shop"*

**The pattern:** Nobody uses the phrase "training intelligence system." But they don't uniformly describe it as a "fitness app" either. Descriptions fall into three buckets:

1. **Feature description:** tracks HYROX, builds plans (Alex, Martin, Anthony, Austin)
2. **Comparison to competitors:** better than Strava, Runna, Whoop (Krysty, Anthony, Austin)
3. **Coaching/intelligence description:** adaptive, monitors, coaches (Andre, Krysty, Dave)

**The real finding:** Users who engage deeply arrive at coaching/intelligence language on their own (Krysty's "integrates sleep and HRV into the plan that adapts" is the cleanest example). Users who don't engage deeply describe it by surface features. The intelligence layer is real — but it's something users have to *experience*, not something we can *pitch* upfront.

## Assumption 4 — $25–30/month replaces a $200+/month coach — 10x value

**Status: Falsified as positioning.**

**Signals that refute it:**

- **Martin:** anchored at €3–5/month, up to €7–10 with full features. Caps total spending at €50/month on sport
- **Martin explicitly:** *"50 euros absolute max on sport"*
- **Andre:** has a coach, won't pay for AI replacement, unlikely to ever convert
- **Anthony:** doesn't pay for gym, doesn't pay for coaching, doesn't describe WTP for Splyts
- **Krysty:** paying for F45 (~$150–200/month equivalent) + Runna + various apps. Has WTP budget but never described Splyts as "replacing my coach"

**Signals that support the original hypothesis:**

- **Austin** (non-adopter): theoretical only — said yes in concept, never activated
- **Dave** (friend, biased): theoretical only

**The real finding:** The "replace your $200 coach" framing pulls in the wrong customers. People who want to save money on coaching think about it that way. People who actually *use* Splyts (like Krysty) think about it differently — as a training system, not as a coach replacement. Retention and engagement don't correlate with coach-replacement intent.

**Reframed finding:** The product was designed for competitive HYROX athletes, but the users who actually adopt and engage are **committed athletes new to HYROX** — not casual beginners, and not established competitors.

# The New Positioning

Based on what survived falsification, our positioning has shifted from the previous two iterations.

## Previous Iterations

- **V1:** "HYROX mode on Apple Watch" — attracted low willingness-to-pay users
- **V2:** "Training intelligence company" — company-inward language, didn't resonate with athletes

## Current Positioning (V3)

**Short form (for a website hero or tagline):**

> Splyts is the training system for athletes who run and lift.

**Medium form (for ads and social posts):**

> Splyts is the training system for athletes who run and lift. It reads your body — your sleep, your recovery, your training — and builds a plan that adapts to you every day.

**Long form (for deeper content):**

> Every workout affects the next. Your sleep shapes your training. Your training shapes your recovery. But until now, nothing has connected them. Splyts captures every signal from your wrist, scores it with sport science, and coaches you based on what's actually happening in your body. Built for athletes who run and lift.

## What Changed

- **Removed "training intelligence"** — company-inward language
- **Removed "replaces your $200 coach"** — falsified positioning that attracts the wrong users
- **Removed "HYROX mode on Apple Watch"** — attracts low-WTP users
- **Added "reads your body and adapts"** — direct echo of Krysty's own language
- **Named the real insight** — every workout affects the next; nothing connects them

# The New Null Hypothesis

> Athletes who run and lift — with a specific training goal they're working toward — lack a system that reads their body and adapts their training accordingly. They have plenty of tools that track one thing (runs, lifts, sleep, recovery) but nothing that connects those signals into a plan that changes based on what they actually did yesterday, how they actually slept, and how their body is actually responding. They're self-directing their training because no tool does this, they're patching together programs from multiple sources, and they can't tell if the mix is working.
>
> Splyts wins by being the training system that reads every signal from the wrist, scores it against validated sport science, and uses AI to adapt the plan daily and deliver coaching the athlete will actually trust and follow. Starting with HYROX as the beachhead — where the running-and-lifting combination is most acute and the market is growing fastest — and expanding into other multimodal contexts as the product matures. Priced at $25–35/month, replacing fragmented fitness tools and substituting for the coach most users won't hire.

## The Assumptions This Hypothesis Contains

Each assumption is drawn from the hypothesis statement itself. The next round of testing targets the HYROX subset of this broader thesis.

### Assumption 1 — HYROX athletes who run and lift are a real, findable market

**Falsified if:** Targeted acquisition through HYROX channels doesn't generate meaningfully more signups than the broader Reddit post, or signups don't match the run-and-lift profile.

### Assumption 2 — The target HYROX athlete has a specific training goal

**Falsified if:** Most HYROX signups have no race booked, no goal they can articulate, or are just "exploring."

### Assumption 3 — HYROX athletes lack a system that reads their body and adapts

**Falsified if:** HYROX signups describe their pain as surface tracking issues (splits, HYROX mode on watch, generic plans) rather than adaptation, or articulate no pain at all.

### Assumption 4 — HYROX athletes' existing tools are fragmented and don't connect signals

**Falsified if:** HYROX signups feel well-served by current tool combinations, or their class-based programming fills the gap completely.

### Assumption 5 — HYROX athletes are self-directing and uncertain about their mix

**Falsified if:** Most HYROX signups have coaches they trust, or don't care whether their training is "working" at a measurable level.

### Assumption 6 — Splyts delivers the adaptive experience for HYROX athletes

**Falsified if:** Activated HYROX users at week 2 describe Splyts as "a training app with a plan" rather than in adaptive language. Or activation rates for HYROX signups stay below 40% within 7 days.

### Assumption 7 — HYROX athletes will trust and follow AI-delivered coaching

**Falsified if:** Activated users ignore the morning brief or override daily recommendations; interviews reveal explicit trust barriers; users use the product for tracking but ignore the coaching layer.

### Assumption 8 — HYROX is the right beachhead

**Falsified if (this round, proxy):** HYROX signups don't activate meaningfully better than the current beta cohort's overall baseline. A stronger falsification — comparing HYROX vs. non-HYROX multimodal — requires a later round.

### Assumption 9 — $25–35/month is the right price for HYROX athletes

**Falsified if:** Activated HYROX users anchor willingness-to-pay below $15/month, or survey data shows fewer than 40% willing to pay $25+.

Krysty is not casual. She trains six days a week with structure, tracks her data, and treats her first solo HYROX as a meaningful goal. She's not trying to "get fit" — she's trying to execute a specific race.

### She's new to HYROX, not new to training

This is the critical distinction. Krysty has a six-year F45 foundation and genuine athletic commitment. She's a beginner in HYROX specifically, but she's an experienced, self-directed athlete overall. The product works for her because it meets sophisticated training needs without assuming she already knows how to periodize for HYROX.

### She wants a system, not a coach

She's never hired a coach. She isn't looking for one now. She wants tooling that respects her intelligence and gives her structure she can trust. Her own words: *"Really it was the integration on AI sort of like more proactively monitoring sleep HRV all of those things and integrating that into the training... for me I need to listen to my body more."*

### She's body-aware

She articulates concerns about her menstrual cycle affecting training, her recovery affecting her mix, her history of *"frequent dumb injuries"* from under-recovery. She wants training to adapt to her body — not a static plan that ignores what's happening day-to-day.

### She trusts the system

Unlike other beta users (Andre, Austin) who expressed AI-coaching skepticism, Krysty follows Splyts' recommendations daily. She has treated the product as her coach. Her engagement (2x daily, detailed feedback) is the proof.

### She wants forward visibility

The one thing she wishes for: *"planning the week or like planning looking forward in the next weeks... for me I am a planner and so when I'm looking at my weeks especially because I do work full-time it's like I kind of have to adjust timewise."* She's not casual about logistics.

## What She's Not

Defining her by what she isn't is as important as defining who she is.

- **Not a casual beginner.** She's serious, structured, committed. "Beginner" only describes her HYROX experience, not her training identity
- **Not an established HYROX competitor.** She's not trying to qualify for Worlds or beat a previous time. It's her first solo race
- **Not a coached athlete.** She's never hired one and isn't looking
- **Not a class-only trainee.** F45 is her strength base, but she trains solo outside class too
- **Not a Garmin user.** Apple Watch is her default
- **Not casually curious.** She signed up for a specific reason, tried to understand the product, and kept using it
- **Not price-insensitive** but not price-anchored either. Already pays for Runna, F45 ($150–200/month equivalent), Strava
- **Not looking for a tracker.** She already has trackers. She's looking for a system
